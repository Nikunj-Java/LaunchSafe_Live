# LaunchSafe Live: instructor run-of-show

## Before learners arrive

Run `python3 server.py`. Open the complete private host link from the terminal.
Open the separate projector page in a second window. Keep your controls and
answer key off the projected display.

Scan the actual runtime QR from one phone on the classroom network. Select a
team and confirm the viewer opens. Test one published announcement. Only then
invite the whole room to join.

Use five teams for 23 learners: three teams of five and two teams of four.
Choose Arena scoring for this edition's lower cash cap and all-missed penalty,
or Original for the uploaded game's original scoring. Explain the selected
profile before starting; it cannot be changed mid-game.

## Opening line

"You are not trying to buy everything. You are trying to make decisions you can
still defend when the launch starts going wrong. Your phone is the dashboard;
your team is the decision-maker. All bids happen out loud."

Point to the budget, three-purchase limit, limited copies and selected scoring
profile. Explain that prices are recorded by you, not submitted by phones.

## Suggested rhythm for each round

| Phase | Your move | Their job |
|---|---|---|
| Brief: 2-3 minutes | Show the scenario and start the discussion timer. | Choose priority risks and set walk-away prices. |
| Auction: 6-8 minutes | Select one lot, publish bids, call the hammer, record SOLD. | Debate the trade-off and bid aloud. |
| Incidents: about 2 minutes | Lock purchases and reveal one card at a time. | Check what was protected and what was accepted. |
| Board: about 4 minutes | Spotlight each team for 45 seconds. | Defend the choice and name its limitation. |
| Podium: about 1 minute | Bank once and reveal the round winner. | Compare the progressive standings; rotate roles. |

These are pacing suggestions, not automatic game deadlines. Timers never change
phases or record actions without you.

## Make all 23 people participate

Use the role cards in the viewer. The risk detective identifies likely pain;
the finance lead sets spending limits; the auction voice calls offers; the board
spokesperson explains the decision; the devil's advocate names what the purchase
will not solve. With four players, combine auction voice and spokesperson.

Ask people to rotate after each round. A different teammate should speak at the
board. Give quieter teammates time to consult before the spokesperson answers.
Optional member names, entered as a comma-separated list, rotate the displayed
role suggestions from round to round.

## Make the auction lively without losing control

"Two copies. Five teams. Who needs this enough to pay for it?"

Show one lot. Let teams confer for a few seconds, then ask for the opening bid.
Select the leading team, enter the price and press Publish live bid. The public
price does not change merely because you are typing.

"Going once. Finance lead, is that still inside your plan? Going twice... SOLD."

Record the sale and confirm it. Cash changes only now. The second copy is a new
auction: do not assume the same price or award it automatically to a runner-up.

To keep the game moving, a no-bid lot can be passed and reopened later. Never
award a fourth purchase or a duplicate item, even for a good joke. The server
will refuse it. Use Receipts & corrections for a genuine entry mistake while
the auction is still open.

## Make the incident reveal count

Close the auction only after everyone has checked the receipts. Remind them:
"No purchases now. These incidents were sealed before we started bidding."

Reveal one card, then pause. Do not immediately click through the whole deck.
Ask one team: "Was that risk accepted deliberately, or did you simply miss it?"
The headline creates drama, but the exact original risk and scoring mapping
remain visible underneath.

## Award the pitch bonus consistently

A +5 pitch should contain a defensible choice, an acknowledged limitation, and
a plausible fallback or evidence requirement. Enter a short reason, visible in
the score breakdown. The bonus is once per team per round, not per purchased
mitigation or per speech.

Useful contrast:

Weak: "We bought it because it sounded important."

Stronger: "We prioritised the unstable payment integration, knowingly accepted
minor UI change risk, and would defer that UI work rather than compromise the
release. Our fallback would still need testing."

A team can receive a pitch bonus even after a bad outcome if its reasoning was
credible. Do not reward only the teams that got lucky with the random incidents.

## Closing line

"Your score tells us what happened in this simulation. Your explanation tells
us whether your team understood the trade-off. What one habit would you take
back to a real delivery team?"

Bank the final round, then click Crown the final champions. Equal totals share
the winning position. Export the scores and a native backup before resetting.

## Five things to avoid

- Do not share the private host URL or project the answer-key tab.
- Do not silently switch between Arena and Original scoring.
- Do not close the auction while a disputed receipt remains unresolved.
- Do not restore a secret backup to change an unfavourable incident draw.
- Do not assume a successful laptop preview proves phones can reach the server.
